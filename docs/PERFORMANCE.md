# Performance log

Rule: never optimise from intuition; record measurements here with date, machine, build and method.

## Targets
60 fps @1080p on a mid-range Windows gaming PC; <400-600 draw calls; capped shadow casters; pooled particles/decals/projectiles;
no per-frame garbage in hot loops; bounded physics bodies/ragdolls; bounded memory. Server: tick < 33 ms budget with large headroom.

### Why boot was slow, and what changed
A CPU profile of boot shows `WebGLProgram.getUniforms` -> `onFirstUse` (the wait for a program to be linked) at **6.2 s of 9.8** on `low`, and `getContext` (creating the GPU process's context) at 1.1-1.3 s. On SwiftShader every program costs about 70-160 ms to link whatever its size, there are 45-100 of them, and this build has **no `KHR_parallel_shader_compile`**, so `compileAsync` degrades to the sequential path here (it helps on real browsers; it is called before a session's first frame and raced against a 20 s timeout). Levers that did work:
- **Door first.** `bootGame` builds the front door's DOM and audio hooks, then yields a paint, then (after 80 ms) makes the stage, controls, creator and figure, then (only if the preset wants it and nobody has clicked in 500 ms) builds the camp behind the door **hidden, with its shaders linked before it is revealed**. Clicking New campaign / Join cancels the backdrop from the click, not from the session. The door's first build of the camp used to cost ~3 s of main thread that the session then threw away and paid again (the door's lights, sun + rim + fill, give different programs from the game's single sun).
- **`renderer.debug.checkShaderErrors`** (per-preset `shaderChecks`, `?shaderchecks=0|1`): three.js asks for the info log of the program and both shaders the first time a program is used, three blocking round trips each (~75 ms each under load here). Off: `playableS` 24-25 -> 16.0 on `low`, 13.4-14.3 -> 8.7-9.2 on `test`. Players keep the checks (a real driver answers in microseconds; the error log is worth having in bug reports); `test` turns them off and verifies once, later.
- **`buildWorld` builds the new world before disposing the old one**, so programs both use stay linked instead of being destroyed and relinked (a live preset change no longer recompiles what did not change).
- The idle scene no longer uploads buffers: `ShotFx` (a dozen pooled buffers, ~30 KB/frame) and `Projectiles` (96 matrices) skip their loops and uploads when nothing is alive and nothing was last frame. A 6 s CPU profile of steady state on `low` shows the main thread 91% idle and ~100 ms of JS in total: **the frame rate is entirely GPU-process time**, nothing allocates or rebuilds per frame that shows up.

## Server soak and tick budget (2026-10-01, D-036)
Method (repeatable): `node scripts/soak.mjs --rooms 4 --bots 4 --npcs 24 --seconds 300 --scenario mixed --out soak.json` (exit 1 when a budget in `packages/shared/src/budgets.ts` is broken; `CB_BUDGET_SLACK=2` doubles
the time budgets on a noisy host; `.github/workflows/soak.yml` runs it by dispatch and nightly). `runSoak` (`apps/server/src/bots/soak/`) builds the real server in process on :2583 (never :2567) and creates real rooms; each room is
**Kessar with the Society's town at the crossing** (`outpost:town`: its colliders and nav grid, the bots' collision worlds rebuilt as the browser would), the garrison, the Syndicate's camp and padding rows up to 24 NPC rows (kept at 24: rows
that fall are replaced), a harnessed wagon and two horses, a file store in a temp directory (every join, leave and outcome is a real save), and 4 bots (`Bot`: the browser's prediction wiring over a real WebSocket) looping patrol, skirmish
(walk to the south bank, shoot the nearest hostile row with the rifle) and ride (mount, gallop, dismount), one bot per room leaving and rejoining every 60 s. Ticks are timed INSIDE the room's own fixed step (`tickProbe`, attributed to the room whose clock
ticked last), the patch encode by wrapping `broadcastPatch`, the wire by counting frames at the server's `ws` sockets (down) and the bots' sockets (up) before the kernel, the heap as `heapUsed + external` (the physics engine's WASM
memory is in `external`) after a forced GC, sampled every 10 s: the growth is the least-squares trend of the second half. **Limits, stated so nobody over-reads the table:** the NPCs hold fire (a bot shot down is hauled to the landing by
the room, and that deliberate teleport would read as a netcode error), so NPC rounds against players are NOT exercised, while bot rounds against the garrison (bleeding, dismemberment) are; the bots share the process and the thread with
the rooms (the tick figures are exact, a whole-process CPU figure is not claimed); a short run's heap trend is noise (`tickBudget.test.ts` does not judge it); bytes are WebSocket payloads without frame headers (add ~6-10 bytes a frame).

**Budgets tightened to what was measured (`SERVER_BUDGET`; contract tests only allow tightening to values their fixtures still pass):** tick p50 5 -> 3 ms, p95 12 -> 8, p99 20 -> 12 (measured 0.67 / 0.96 / 1.16; the rest is headroom for shared CI, which `CB_BUDGET_SLACK` multiplies);

a rider's dismount is a deliberate 1.0-1.18 m server placement beside the horse (the mount tests allow 1.5 for it), so a soak whose bots ride cannot meet 1.00; walking, shooting and joining add nothing (the position drift the bots see is 6e-5 m). `judgeSoak` also gained an optional

the trend became 9 MB/h. A one-off world rebuild or a join is the only thing that has made a tick exceed ~3 ms (max 7.8 ms). `/metrics` now reports `tick.p50Ms/p95Ms/p99Ms` and `sections` (per-section avg and p95 since the process started).

## Merged crowd LOD (2026-10-01, D-036)
**What it is.** A `buildCharacter(spec, { merged: true })` rig draws its mid and far crowd levels as ONE `SkinnedMesh` each, every vertex weighted 1.0 to the rig's own joint (the Groups ARE the skeleton: the animator, IK and trade poses are untouched; `packages/procedural/src/three/merged.ts`).
Level 1 carries a frozen neutral face (eyes open, brows and mouth at rest; no blinks or brows at 20-45 m); level 0 (ink, faces, hands) is exactly as before. The villagers (`FolkBody`) use it; wounds, stumps and hulls are not shown at levels 1-2 of a merged rig (scenery).
Triangles do not change: the merged mesh has the vertices and triangles of the parts it replaces (tested on all 22 roster people at both levels).
